What the forecast track predicts

Unlike Stage 1 (row-level allowed amount) and Stage 2 (row-level OOP), the forecast track predicts an aggregate: the mean allowed amount per (provider_type, hcpcs_bucket, state) group in each of 2024, 2025, 2026. There are 20,572 such groups with enough history to forecast. Evaluation is on a 2022-2023 temporal holdout, aggregated to 32,481 group-year observations (16,240 unique groups, roughly 2 years each).

Forecast R² and Stage 1 R² are not directly comparable. Stage 1 R² is on millions of individual HCPCS rows. Forecast R² is on thousands of smoother group-year means. A forecast R² of 0.885 is not "worse than" a Stage 1 R² of 0.943; they answer different questions. PROGRESS.md calls this out explicitly.

The teacher-forcing bug that changed everything

The reported V1 LSTM metric was R² 0.886, RMSE $36.42. When I was building the stacker in V2_12, I needed the LSTM autoregressive predictions (not teacher-forced) to use as a feature. That is when I discovered modeling/train_lstm_local.py:340 evaluate() uses 1-step-ahead teacher forcing, not autoregressive rollout. During eval, the function feeds the TRUE value at each position and asks the model to predict one step ahead. So when "predicting" 2023, the LSTM sees the TRUE 2022 value. At actual inference time (2024 to 2026 forecast), the LSTM has to feed its own predictions back and compounding error produces meaningfully worse numbers.

The RMSE difference of $17.51 is large enough to invalidate the entire V2_09 through V2_11 analysis. My earlier writeups described "LSTM has heavy right tail, Chronos has half the RMSE" as if it were a real property. It was a measurement artifact. Under fair comparison, all base models have RMSE/MAE ratios between 1.9 and 2.1, which is near-Gaussian. There was no "Chronos catches the tails the LSTM misses" effect.

V2_12 Cell 6 contains a working batched autoregressive rollout that can be adapted to fix evaluate(). The bug is still in the code as of 2026-04-23 and is backlog item #1 in MODELING.md.

Architecture of the winner: LGB Stacker V2_12

The stacker is a LightGBM meta-learner over two base models (LSTM V1 and Chronos-Bolt cpi_cf_deflated) plus history conditioning features. It is self-contained: colab/V2_12_stacker_forecast.ipynb retrains the LSTM in-notebook (~5 min on A100) and re-runs Chronos inference (~2 min) before training the stacker.

Why it wins at all

Chronos contributes only 3.6% of stacker gain. The stacker is essentially LSTM (70.5%) plus a persistence anchor (16.0%) plus history conditioning (~10%). Two effects are in play. First, prediction-level diversity: LSTM and Chronos disagree on which specific group-years are hard, and blending cancels independent errors. Second, history conditioning: the stacker learns when to trust each base model based on history_cv, history_trend, n_history_years.

Why the lift is capped at +0.016 R²

Under fair comparison, base models have near-identical error distributions (RMSE/MAE ratios 1.9 to 2.1 across the board). The "complementary error profiles" hypothesis from the V2_09 through V2_11 writeups was wrong; that was the teacher-forcing artifact. True diversity between LSTM and Chronos is limited.

5-fold GroupKFold

CV splits on (ptype, bucket, state) hash so a group's 2022 observation never sits in train while its 2023 observation sits in test. OOF predictions give unbiased metrics. Final stacker refits on all 2022 to 2023 data and applies to pre-computed 2024 to 2026 LSTM + Chronos forecasts.

Why TFT lost (both times) and what that taught me

TFT appeared twice. V2_06 was univariate (target only), hit R² 0.846, and lost to LSTM. V2_13 was multivariate (5 observed + 3 known-future covariates), hit R² 0.8691, and tied fair LSTM but lost to the stacker by 0.016.

VariantInputsR²Interpretation
V2_06 (univariate)Target only, 6-year encoder0.846Architecture was fine; input was starved
V2_13 (multivariate)5 observed + 3 known-future decoder inputs0.8691Tied LSTM; multivariate hypothesis rejected

The V2_13 multivariate TFT with log_srvcs, log_benes, Avg_Sbmtd_Chrg, Bene_Avg_Risk_Scre, srvcs_per_bene as time-varying unknowns plus CPI, CF, COVID as known-future decoder inputs is the expensive null result. At annual resolution, these covariates move in near-lockstep with the target. Their past trajectories carry no orthogonal signal beyond what the target's own history provides. The multivariate hypothesis only pays off if the covariates have a different temporal shape than the target, which they do not at this resolution.

Secondary finding: TFT produces more coherent multi-horizon forecasts than the stacker's autoregressive drift. TFT predicts 2024/2025/2026 as $70/$68/$68; the stacker inherits the LSTM's mean-reversion and predicts $72/$62/$62. Arguably the TFT trajectory is more product-credible. I chose the stacker for the 0.016 R² metric gap.

Signal ceiling argument

Three independent architectures (fair LSTM, Chronos-Bolt, multivariate TFT) cluster between 0.857 and 0.869 R² on the 2022 to 2023 holdout. The stacker jumps to 0.8852 by exploiting ensemble diversity. Above that, nothing else tested gains more than noise.

The track closed on 2026-04-14 with the conclusion that annual-resolution Medicare data has a ceiling near R² 0.885. The only remaining lever is finer temporal resolution. Going from 11 annual points to ~44 quarterly points per group would unlock CNN/TCN/PatchTST architectures and meaningful Chronos fine-tuning. That requires a 2 to 4 week data-engineering project to ingest CMS MUP-PS quarterly aggregates (if they exist; not verified).

Schema is drop-in compatible with the existing LSTM forecast table in Supabase, so the frontend forecast explorer did not need changes. Quantile bounds currently collapse to the point prediction; a follow-up is to train 3 LightGBM stackers at alpha={0.1, 0.5, 0.9} for genuine uncertainty bands.

Limitations and what I would do differently

  • 1Fix the teacher-forcing bug in train_lstm_local.py:340. Add a --eval-mode {teacher_forced, autoregressive} flag and report both. The V2_12 rollout code is the drop-in fix.
  • 2Quarterly data is the one lever left to push R² above 0.89. Scope a 2-4 week ingestion project if CMS MUP-PS quarterly aggregates exist.
  • 3Train quantile stackers at alpha 0.1, 0.5, 0.9 so the forecast chart in the frontend shows real uncertainty bands, not duplicated points.
  • 4Fine-tune Chronos-Bolt on Medicare sequences. Zero-shot hit ceiling at 0.8576; even LoRA might close the gap to the stacker.
  • 5Consider shipping TFT V2_13 instead of the stacker for the product UX. TFT's 2024/2025/2026 trajectory ($70/$68/$68) is more credible than the stacker's mean-reverting ($72/$62/$62), even at 0.016 R² lower.

30-second pitch

I tested five architectures for forecasting Medicare allowed amounts three years forward: LSTM V1, univariate TFT, Chronos-T5-Large and Chronos-Bolt foundation models with five deflation and derivation variants, a LightGBM stacker over LSTM plus Chronos, and a multivariate TFT with five observed covariates plus CPI as a known-future decoder input. The stacker won at R² 0.8852 on the 2022 to 2023 holdout, and the multivariate TFT tied LSTM at 0.869 which confirmed a signal ceiling around 0.885 at annual resolution. While building the stacker I discovered a teacher-forcing bug in the LSTM evaluate function that had inflated earlier metrics by 0.017 R² and $17.51 RMSE, which invalidated my interpretations from the Chronos experiments. The track closed on 2026-04-14 with the conclusion that only quarterly data ingestion can push R² meaningfully higher.
