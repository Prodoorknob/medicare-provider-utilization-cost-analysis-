What this project is

AllowanceMap is an end-to-end Medicare cost prediction system. It takes a (specialty, state, HCPCS bucket, place of service) request and returns the predicted Medicare allowed amount, plus a P10/P50/P90 patient out-of-pocket distribution conditioned on demographic context, plus a three-year projection of allowed amounts at the specialty by state by clinical-bucket level.

The work was structured as eight phases. Phases 1 through 6 built the data pipeline, V1 models, and the deployed web app. Phase 7 was a full-data retrain on Colab Pro that produced V2 (the production stack). Phase 8 was the forecasting closeout (Chronos foundation models, multivariate TFT, the LightGBM stacker) which determined an empirical signal ceiling at annual resolution.

The personal goal was to work at insurance-sector data scale (over 100M rows for ML), explore the breadth of tabular boosters and time-series architectures honestly, and ship something a non-technical user could open and click.

The two-stage framing matters. Stage 1 predicts what Medicare allows. Stage 2 takes that allowed amount and predicts what the patient pays. They are independent models, but the connection is mechanical: Stage 1 output is Stage 2 feature #1.

Tech stack and why

LayerChoiceWhy this and not the obvious alternative
StorageParquet, per-state filesColumnar pruning matters at 126M rows. Per-state sharding lets the Phase 1 batch trainers stream by Census region under a 24 GB WSL2 RAM budget. Single Delta table would have forced Spark locally, which was overkill.
Pipeline runtimePlain Python on local + Databricks parityEach notebook ships in two flavors, notebooks/0X_*.py for Databricks and notebooks/0X_*_local.py for pandas. Same logic, different I/O. Avoided Airflow because the schedule is "one-shot reproducible".
Stage 1 modelLightGBM (GOSS, leaf-wise)Beat XGBoost (R² 0.9575 vs 0.9452) and CatBoost (0.9070) on the same 126.8M rows. Native categorical handling avoided one-hot blowup for HCPCS_Cd_idx (~6K unique).
Stage 2 modelCatBoost monotonic V2 + asymmetric CQRV1 XGBoost quantile beats V2 on point R² (0.40 vs 0.17) but lacks monotonicity guarantees. V2 CatBoost ships with a CQR sidecar (oop_calibration.json) that widens raw P90 from 67.5% to nominal 90% marginal coverage at inference. Calibration constants reproduce V2_04's MLflow log to 4.6e-5.
ForecastLightGBM stacker over LSTM + ChronosBeat fair LSTM by 0.016 R² and any single foundation model by ~0.03. Multivariate TFT tied LSTM but lost to the stacker.

Honest limitations to volunteer up front

  • 1Stage 2 is trained on synthetic data. Real OOP came from generate_synthetic_mcbs.py with hardcoded modulation factors (-85% dual eligible, -35% supplemental). Real MCBS LDS is $600 per module + DUA.
  • 2The reported V1 LSTM R² of 0.886 was teacher-forced. Fair autoregressive eval is 0.8689. The Phase 8 work surfaced and corrected this; the bug still exists in modeling/train_lstm_local.py:340.
  • 3CMS specialty names are inconsistent across years. Cardiology splits into idx 16 and 17 because the 2016 file uses a different string. Confirmed for three specialties; full audit not run.
  • 4No cross-validation on Stage 1 V1. Single 80/20 split with seed 42. V2 ensemble used 5-fold CV but lift over LightGBM was only +0.0004 R² for 13.3 hours of compute.

30-second pitch

I built a two-stage Medicare cost prediction system on 126.8 million CMS rows: Stage 1 predicts the allowed amount with a LightGBM trained on 13 features and reaches R² 0.94 without using the billed charge as a near-leaky input; Stage 2 takes that allowed amount and predicts patient out-of-pocket as P10, P50, P90 quantiles using monotonic CatBoost. There is also an independent forecast track where I tested LSTM, Chronos foundation models, and a multivariate TFT, and a LightGBM stacker over LSTM plus Chronos won at R² 0.885 and is in production. The whole thing serves real-time predictions through FastAPI on Railway, with precomputed lookups in Supabase, behind a Next.js frontend on Vercel.
