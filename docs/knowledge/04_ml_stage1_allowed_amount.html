What Stage 1 actually predicts

Target is Avg_Mdcr_Alowd_Amt, the dollar amount Medicare allows for a given (specialty, state, HCPCS code, place of service) combination. Each row in the gold parquet is one such combination for a specific NPI in a specific year. The model predicts the per-service allowed amount on a log1p scale and inverts at predict time.

This is not a small-data problem. V2 trains on 126.8 million rows with a 13-feature input. The original V1 used a 30 percent sample plus regional batching to fit under a 24 GB WSL2 memory budget; full data in V2 was the single biggest unlock and added +0.07 R² on its own.

The previous spec target was Avg_Mdcr_Pymt_Amt (payment). It got swapped to allowed amount on 2026-04-04 because Stage 2 needs to take what Medicare allows and predict what the patient pays from there. Allowed is the upstream of the cost-sharing calculation, payment is the downstream.

The non-obvious choices

Why LightGBM not XGBoost

At full national scale on 126.8M rows, LightGBM beat XGBoost by +0.012 R² with 50 percent of XGBoost's wall-clock time, and beat CatBoost by +0.05 R² with one third of its training time. The credit goes to GOSS (gradient-based one-side sampling: keeps every large-gradient instance plus a random sample of small-gradient ones, which converges faster on heterogeneous data) and leaf-wise tree growth (best-first, deeper effective trees per iteration than XGBoost's level-wise). LightGBM's native categorical support also avoided one-hot blowup for HCPCS_Cd_idx (~6K unique codes). Hyperparameters in modeling/train_lgbm_local.py:97-111.

Why we serve the no-charge variant despite -0.015 R²

In V1, Avg_Sbmtd_Chrg had RF feature importance 0.618, dominating the model. The hypothesis going into V2_02 was that this was near-leakage; predicted ablation cost was -0.25 to -0.30 R². Actual cost was -0.015. Two features (hcpcs_target_enc and HCPCS_Cd_idx) capture the same pricing signal via procedure identity, so removing the charge column barely hurts. The no-charge variant is what gets served in api/models/loader.py:77 because the API user does not know the billed charge at request time. Production accepts the small accuracy hit for the realistic input contract.

Why the ensemble was not deployed

5-fold OOF stacking with a Ridge meta-learner over XGB, CatBoost, LightGBM produced R² 0.9580, which is +0.0005 over LightGBM alone. Ridge weights came back as LGB=1.120, XGB=-0.097, CB=-0.023, meaning the meta-learner essentially picked LightGBM. Total cost was 13.3 hours of A100 (~149 CU). The base models had near-identical error patterns on the same feature set, so blending bought no diversity. This is one of the cleanest "ensembles do not always help" datapoints in the project.

Why log1p target transform

Allowed amounts span four orders of magnitude (an E&M visit is ~$80, a specialized procedure can be $5,000+). Without log1p, RMSE on raw dollars makes the loss surface dominated by the high-cost outliers and the model under-predicts everything else. Inverse via np.expm1 at predict time, with a max(0, .) clip in the API at api/services/prediction.py:167 because the model can produce small negative values on hard cases.

Why a single 80/20 random split, not k-fold

Honest answer: time and compute. The V2 ensemble already cost 13.3 hours per fold-set. A proper 5-fold CV on the 80/20 data would have multiplied that. The cost was metric-variance opacity: I do not know the standard deviation on R²=0.9428. This is acknowledged in the critique report and listed as a backlog item.

The booster file api/models/artifacts/lgbm_v2_no_charge.txt ships with the API container. Loader prefers no-charge over full-charge over lgbm_model.txt (legacy) at api/models/loader.py:77-82.

Limitations and what I would do differently

  • 1Run k-fold CV (or at least a 5-seed retrain) to get metric variance. R² 0.9428 is a point estimate from a single split; I cannot defend the third decimal.
  • 2Random 80/20 split leaks across years — train rows can be 2023 and test rows can be 2013. A temporal split (train ≤ 2021, test 2022-2023) would test whether models generalize to future years; this is what the LSTM does already.
  • 3Replace Avg_Sbmtd_Chrg with the actual MPFS RVU components (Work, PE, MP) plus GPCI. This converts the model from "learn the fee schedule" to "learn deviations from the fee schedule" which is a more publishable framing.
  • 4Specialty rename canonicalization — the Cardiology / Colorectal / Oral Surgery split is real and silently degrades model quality for those rows.
  • 5The CatBoost V2 result (0.9070) is suspiciously low; I think 3000 iterations was not enough convergence for the GPU build with bootstrap_type='MVS'. Worth a rerun before final claims.

  • The no-charge ablation as the central modeling decision — predicted -0.30 R², actual -0.015
  • Why GOSS plus leaf-wise made LightGBM win over XGBoost on this data shape
  • The negative ensemble result: 5-fold Ridge stack added +0.0004 R² for 13.3 hrs of A100, Ridge picked LightGBM with weight 1.12
  • Single-split risk: I do not have CV variance, so I quote R² to 4 decimals but only defend it to 2

Volunteer to a data scientist

  • Stage 1 R² is on individual HCPCS rows; do not compare it to forecast R² on group-year aggregates
  • The leakage cleanup (drop Avg_Mdcr_Pymt_Amt, Avg_Mdcr_Stdzd_Amt) that took the V0 toy R² from 0.9996 to where the real work could start
  • HCPCS target encoding alongside raw HCPCS_Cd_idx: continuous + categorical of the same field, lets the model split either way

30-second pitch

Stage 1 is a LightGBM regressor on 126.8 million CMS rows that predicts the Medicare allowed amount per service. I trained six model families across V1 and V2; LightGBM with GOSS and leaf-wise growth won on full data at R² 0.9575 with the billed charge included, R² 0.9428 without it. Production is the no-charge variant because the API user cannot know the submitted charge at request time, and the ablation cost is only 0.015 R² since procedure identity captures most of the pricing signal anyway. I tried a 5-fold OOF ensemble with a Ridge meta-learner and it bought +0.0004 R² for 13.3 hours of A100, which I document as a clean negative result rather than ship.
