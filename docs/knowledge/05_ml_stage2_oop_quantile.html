Why Stage 2 exists at all

Stage 1 tells the user what Medicare allows. That is not what the user actually pays. The Medicare cost-sharing formula is roughly: deductible until met, then 20 percent coinsurance on most Part B services, modified by Medigap or Medicaid coverage, modulated by income-related premium surcharges. Two beneficiaries getting the same procedure from the same provider can pay wildly different OOP based on demographics and supplemental coverage.

Stage 2 takes the Stage 1 prediction as feature #1 and predicts a P10/P50/P90 distribution of patient OOP conditioned on demographic context. It is the answer to "what will this cost me".

Stage 1 and Stage 2 are independent models. They share no parameters. The connection is mechanical: Stage 1 output is Stage 2 input feature #1 (Avg_Mdcr_Alowd_Amt). The API stitches them together at api/routers/predict.py:99-170.

The synthetic data problem

Real OOP data needs the MCBS Limited Data Set (LDS). LDS costs $600 per module + DUA + 6 to 8 weeks of CMS processing. The free public PUF has three constraints I cover in Report 02 that make per-service OOP impossible: no Census region, non-overlapping IDs across the Survey and Cost files, and OOP is annual not per-service.

So I generate synthetic per-service OOP from real distributions. generate_synthetic_mcbs.py samples demographics from the real MCBS Cost Supplement (age, sex, income, chronic count) and pairs them with real provider gold rows. OOP is then computed as Avg_Mdcr_Alowd_Amt * 0.20 baseline (the 20 percent coinsurance) and modulated by:

ModulatorEffectSource
Dual eligible (Medicare + Medicaid)OOP × 0.15 (-85% reduction)Literature estimate, hardcoded
Has supplemental insurance (Medigap)OOP × 0.65 (-35% reduction)Literature estimate, hardcoded
Chronic condition count+3% per condition above baseline of 1Hardcoded

Output is local_pipeline/mcbs_synthetic/synthetic_oop.parquet, ~10.3M rows. The crosswalk script (notebooks/08_mcbs_crosswalk_local.py) supports both --mode puf (real, national) and --mode synthetic (this generator). A researcher with real LDS data can drop in their own parquet with the same schema and the rest of the Stage 2 pipeline works unchanged.

Why V2 lost on OOP (the honest story)

V2_04 CatBoost Monotonic + CQR

The hypothesis was strong. CatBoost supports per-feature monotone_constraints, so I encoded five domain priors directly: allowed amount up implies OOP up, income up implies OOP up (less subsidy), chronic count up implies OOP up, dual eligibility implies OOP down, supplemental implies OOP down. Three quantiles (P10, P50, P90) were trained as separate boosters with post-hoc sort enforcing non-crossing. Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) with a 60/20/20 calibration split was supposed to give nominal 80 percent interval coverage.

Result: P50 R² 0.173, worse than V1 XGB Quantile (0.40). Root cause is that the synthetic data does not perfectly obey the priors I encoded. The hardcoded modulation factors (-85% dual, -35% supplemental) are literature estimates, not learned from claims. When monotonicity constraints fight the data distribution, the model trades fit for shape correctness.

Note: CatBoost GPU does not support monotone_constraints, so this trained on CPU. P10 early-stopped at 5 iterations because the quantile loss flattened at the floor (most P10 values are near $0). P50 needed 4000 iterations at LR=0.1 to converge.

V2_05 Zero-Inflated

The hypothesis: dual-eligible beneficiaries pay $0 OOP for many services, so the OOP distribution is genuinely bimodal. A two-stage model should outperform a single regressor. Stage A is a CatBoost classifier predicting P(OOP=0), Stage B is a conditional regression on non-zero OOP. Prediction is (1 - p_zero) * predicted_oop.

Result: P50 R² -0.054, worse than predicting the mean. The gate classifier and conditional regression compound errors; a misclassification on the gate gets multiplied by a regression error in the wrong direction. The synthetic data also does not have the clean zero-inflation pattern I hypothesized because the modulation factors smooth out the zero-mass.

The lesson: do not over-engineer on proxy data. Both V2 architectures (monotonic, zero-inflated) are well-motivated for the real OOP distribution. They lose on synthetic data because the proxy data does not exhibit the structural assumptions they encode. With real MCBS LDS, I would expect monotonic CatBoost to win.

Inference path

build_stage2_features() at api/services/prediction.py:179 - state -> census_region via STATE_TO_REGION dict - Stage 1 prediction passed in as Avg_Mdcr_Alowd_Amt - All categoricals cast to int (CatBoost requirement) ▼ Pool(features, cat_features=[2,3,4,5], feature_names=OOP_FEATURE_NAMES) ▼ artifacts.oop_p10.predict() artifacts.oop_p50.predict() artifacts.oop_p90.predict() ▼ Apply asymmetric CQR (loaded from oop_calibration.json): p10 = p10 - artifacts.oop_q_lo (q_lo = +$0.0004, ~no-op) p90 = p90 + artifacts.oop_q_hi (q_hi = +$14.4710) ▼ Floor at 0, enforce non-crossing: p50 = max(p10, p50); p90 = max(p50, p90) ▼ Stage2Response(p10, p50, p90, census_region)

Two post-prediction steps. Asymmetric CQR widening at api/services/prediction.py:256-257 is the calibration step: raw model P90 marginal coverage is 67.5% (target 90%), the q_hi constant brings it to nominal. P10 is essentially untouched (q_lo ≈ 0) because raw P10 already over-covers slightly. Non-crossing enforcement at api/services/prediction.py:259-262 then floors at zero and ensures p10 ≤ p50 ≤ p90; CatBoost monotonic does not guarantee non-crossing across separate quantile regressors, so post-hoc sort fixes it.

Limitations and what I would do differently

  • 1Get real MCBS LDS data ($600 + DUA). The CatBoost Monotonic V2 architecture would likely revive on real data because the priors it encodes are domain-true; they just do not match the synthetic data distribution.
  • 2Replace hardcoded modulation factors with learned coefficients from the real PUF Cost Supplement. Even at the national aggregate level, fit a regression to derive data-driven dual / supplemental / chronic effects instead of literature estimates.
  • 3Add interaction features explicitly: allowed_amt × dual_eligible and chronic_count × age. Tree models can learn these but explicit features help shallow trees converge faster.
  • 4Done 2026-04-27. Asymmetric CQR sidecar shipped at oop_calibration.json; loader applies q_lo/q_hi at inference. Symmetric q_hat reproduces V2_04's MLflow log to 4.6e-5. Test-set coverage post-calibration: 80.1% interval, 90.0% P90 marginal. Re-run on real LDS data with python modeling/calibrate_oop.py when it arrives.
  • 5Resolved by #4. Production is CatBoost Mono V2 + CQR. The V1-vs-V2 tension was about coverage spec, not point R²; with calibration applied, CatBoost Mono V2 now hits both monotonicity and nominal coverage. V1 XGB Quantile keeps its R² lead on point predictions but is no longer the deployment candidate.

30-second pitch

Stage 2 takes the Stage 1 allowed amount as feature one and predicts patient out-of-pocket as P10, P50, P90 quantiles using CatBoost monotonic quantile regression on 10.3 million synthetic rows, plus an asymmetric Conformalized Quantile Regression sidecar that calibrates the bands at inference time. The synthetic data is necessary because the real MCBS LDS costs $600 per module plus a Data Use Agreement, and the public PUF has three constraints (no Census region, non-overlapping IDs, annual not per-service) that make real per-service OOP impossible without it. The deployed model trades 0.227 R² of point accuracy for monotonicity guarantees in five domain features (allowed amount, income, chronic count, dual eligibility, supplemental coverage), and CQR brings the raw 67.5% P90 marginal coverage up to the nominal 90% by adding a $14.47 widening constant on the upper tail. The calibration was logged to MLflow at training time but not exported as an inference artifact until 2026-04-27 — the production API was serving uncalibrated bands for two weeks, which is documented as a learning lesson on the gap between training-time validation and serving-time behavior.
